Board runs in parallel: the proof (2026-11-25/26)

The proof of section 4 of the plan for parallel board runs (not published), decided on 2026-11-25 (change log). The question was whether the boards give the same numbers when they run at the same time, through the queue (our lab's job queue, lab/tools/boardqueue.py, not published, commit 62ce14e).

Setup:

  • Every job ran kinds/crc/bench/run-crc.py from a clean worktree at commit 40f1958, with one pass and two configurations of the bundle on the board.
  • The comparison was per board, image and scenario, the means of the configuration and of the baseline. "Within" means within 1 % or 0.05 cycles.
  • The run files and logs are kept on the lab host under ~/vl-wt-crc-runs/proof-parallel/ and the queue's jobs, logs and write ledger under ~/vl-queue/; they make no record.

C2, the Pico 2's write times alone, was not measured.

  • What happened: the job was to write bundle 6. The runner found it already on the board, so nothing was written. The job's 8 runs (384 means, identical to the records) passed, and the job failed on its expected write, as it should.
  • Why there was nothing to write: bundles 6 and 7 do not exclude each other on the Pico 2. Bundle 6 spans 0x10000000–0x103ff000 in 226 segments and leaves the range of bundle 7's images empty; both start with the same boot stub. So both have been on the board since the second series, and the count of the job list, which took bundle 7 to have replaced bundle 6 as on the STM32 boards, was wrong.
  • No write for timing only: there is no other Pico 2 bundle at this commit, and a write only to time it was not made. The part of C3 with the Pico 2 writing was dropped with it.

What the numbers support (a reading; the rules are the owner's decision)

  • Runs on several boards at once:
    • Every number of every board was the same as when the board ran alone, to the third decimal but one mean off by 0.004 cycles.
    • The retries were the same.
    • This supports parallel runs.
  • Writes in parallel on one hub:
    • Two STM32 boards wrote at once, and a board ran beside a writing neighbour.
    • Every write verified, each write took within 1.1 s of its time alone, and the runs that followed gave the records' numbers.
    • This supports writes in parallel for the STM32 boards, the SAM D20 and, by the same probe-rs path, the nRF5340, though no nRF5340 write was made.
  • The Pico 2 writing alone: not tested. Its rule rests on the hub cutting its port about 65 s into a write. Nothing here shows whether another board's write lengthens a Pico 2 part. The rule stays until a Pico 2 write is timed beside a neighbour's.
  • Power switching beside a running board: four cycles of a neighbour's port on the same hub changed no number and lost no run. That supports switching a board that is not running beside one that is. The queue's rule, a free hub, is stricter than these four cycles need, but four cycles are few.

What was not measured

  • A Pico 2 write, alone or beside a neighbour's (above).
  • More than four power cycles, and a power cycle during another board's flash write.
  • A write on the nRF5340 DK beside others. Its port is never switched: after a power-on our boot stub leaves its debug port locked.
  • Version 2's runner (firmware/nrf5340-rb-bench/run-one.py) through the queue. Only the CRC kind's runner ran, though both print the same "FLASH WRITE" line the queue counts.
  • Runs longer than about 12 minutes at once. The longest job was the Pico 2's.

Addition: version 2's runner through the queue (2026-11-26, 10:20 to 10:28 UTC)

The trial the decision of 2026-11-26 asks for before ring-buffer runs go on several boards at once (change log), with the reviewer's OK for each job.

  • Setup. All jobs on the NUCLEO-G474RE, from a clean worktree at a3174e7 (main), with firmware/nrf5340-rb-bench/run-one.py on bundle 0 (built there with onebuild.sh table). Two configurations, lwrb and jnk0le, one pass, 16 runs each job.
