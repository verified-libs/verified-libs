Re-run with lwrb v3.4.0: every test at one commit, all six boards (2026-11-21/22)

lwrb released v3.4.0 (b8534fd15925), which adds functions for one byte, lwrb_write_byte and lwrb_read_byte. The pin moved from v3.3.0 (413b7093dcad), and lwrb's single bytes are now measured through the new functions (DECISIONS 2026-11-22). The pin and the adapter are test sources, and a record must name sources the reader has, so everything the site shows was measured again at one commit, a7a206190337, the same way as in the publication re-run: lab/tools/publication-rerun.sh 5 5 on each board (code size, the three benchmark sets at eight code placements, five hours of the ISR stress test, five hours of the two-interrupt test). Start 2026-11-21 23:42 UTC; the last board finished 2026-11-22 12:52 UTC. The method is the frozen one (tag v1-method-freeze-2); no test and no campaign script changed.

Result

  • lwrb v3.4.0: no errors in its 108 runs of the ISR stress test (13 of them with the two sides on the Pico 2's two cores), and the model check passes for the byte functions and for the block calls.
  • lwrb's single bytes are 3 to 4 times faster through the new functions (table below). Its blocks cost what they did within 4 %, and its zero-copy calls within 6 %, most of them a little more than before. Its code grew by 108 to 132 bytes. (Until a correction on 2026-11-22 this line said "within 4 %" for both; two zero-copy numbers on the H723 are 4.3 and 5.4 % higher.)
  • ISR stress test: no errors in any real library in any run (108 runs on six boards, all valid).
  • Two-interrupt test: no errors in any library in its documented mode in any valid run (324 runs on six boards, 322 valid). lwrb is a single-producer, single-consumer library and is not in this test.
  • No step failed: no benchmark image needed a second try, no stress run was repeated, no board was power-cycled.
  • The other libraries' speed numbers moved too, although their code did not change, because all the C and C++ libraries share one benchmark image and its layout changed. Most moves are small; 29 of 666 means left the placement range shown before, and one number moved by 57 % (see "What moved in the other libraries").
  • The two invalid runs of the two-interrupt test are the same two entries of the rotation as on 2026-11-19/20.

Host-side checks with v3.4.0, before any board ran:

  • GenMC (verify/genmc): the byte functions pass (7,118 executions), as do the block calls with a length of 1 (7,118), blocks (730) and zero-copy (8,988).   • The one-core contract check (verify/contract): pass.

Benchmarks

24 images per board (three sets at placement shifts 0, 4, ..., 28), 25 on the nRF5340 (Nordic's nrf_ringbuf, one placement). All 145 ran at the first try. The records are in records/bench/ (151 with the six code-size records).

Only one of the benchmark images changed with the pin: the one with the C and C++ single-producer libraries, which holds lwrb. The Rust image, the multi-producer image and Nordic's were byte for byte those of the publication re-run (built at both commits and compared before the run). They were written and measured again all the same, and gave the same numbers: all 66 means of the Rust libraries and the 5 of Nordic's nrf_ringbuf are identical to the last digit recorded, and so are 716 of the 720 numbers of the multi-producer set (means and range ends); the other four differ by at most 0.03 %. An image that is the same gives the same cycle counts on these boards.

Summed over the library rows of the six campaign reports: 11.6 × 10⁹ bytes through the buffers under 6.3 × 10⁹ interrupts. The interrupt sweep made 114 to 539 million trials per board, with the interrupt inside the call in 76 to 82 % of them.

lwrb v3.4.0 was in every run: 9, 9, 27, 13 and 8 runs on the nRF5340, the G0B1, the G474, the H723 and the SAM D20, and 42 on the Pico 2 (13 of them on two cores, which lwrb documents). No errors. The stress test moves its single bytes through the byte functions too; its zero-copy calls were stressed on the G474, the H723 and the Pico 2.

Errors only where they are expected, as in every campaign before:

  • on every board, the three variants their own projects do not call interrupt-safe: wizard97 as shipped, Arduino RingBufferN as shipped and RT-Thread rt_ringbuffer without a lock;
  • on the Pico 2 with the two sides on two cores, the nine single-core designs (their interrupt lock does nothing against the other core).

Mutants (four buffers with one deliberate defect each): the sweep caught every mutant in every sweep run in the directions where its defect can show, on all six boards. With random interrupts the weak spots are the same as before: "drain re-reads head" was caught in 12 of 27 single-byte runs (11 of 27 on 2026-11-19/20), and "full test misses the wrap" in the direction main to interrupt in 16 of 20 block runs.

What this run says about re-running

Three things were measured here that the project did not know before:

  • The same image gives the same numbers (787 of 791 identical, the rest within 0.03 %), and the two-interrupt test with the same images gave the same run counts, the same valid runs and no errors.
  • A change in one library's code moves the numbers of the libraries that share its image, on the nRF5340 by more than their placement range.
  • Most of what ran had not changed. Of the 24 or 25 benchmark images per board, 16 or 17 were the same (the Rust, multi-producer and Nordic sets). In the stress tests each library runs from its own image: every image of the two-interrupt test, and every image of the ISR test except lwrb's, is byte for byte the one of 2026-11-19/20. Running them again changed nothing on the page beyond the campaigns' new seeds.

So measuring everything again for one library's new version buys little for the images that did not change, and it is what the record rule requires today. A proposal for a rule that keeps the records of unchanged images is in the plan (lab/plans/2026-11-22-same-image-rule.md); it is a proposal, and nothing in the tools has changed.
