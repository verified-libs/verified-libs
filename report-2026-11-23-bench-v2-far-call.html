Benchmark version 2: the series with the far call (2026-11-23)

The series the pages of version 2 are to show. Every library lies at least 6144 bytes behind its adapter (FAR, DECISIONS 2026-11-23), so that a call from the adapter into the library is a far one on every board, as it is in an application where the linker places the library. The first run of version 2, earlier the same day, had the library right behind its adapter (report); its records are replaced by the ones of this series and stay in the history (commit f3d0682).

Result

  • 265 records on six boards (45 on the nRF5340 DK, 44 on each of the others), eight placements and two passes each: 4240 runs, every one with its run file, no configuration left out.
  • Every image rebuilt from the records' commit (d06180e) and compared: 265 of 265 the same bytes (oneverify.sh).
  • Baseline (the same code at the same place in every image of a board): 20 of 20 numbers the same in every run on five boards. On the NUCLEO-H723ZG 19 of 20; the single push with the cache on is 10.014 or 10.024.
  • The NUCLEO-G0B1RE and the SAM D20 now have a record for the configuration the first run could not print there (DNedic lockfree mpmc::Queue; it has no numbers on a Cortex-M0+ and the record says so).

What the distance costs

Far minus near, cycles per call, single push and single pop, the median over all runs of a configuration. "Inlined whole" are the configurations with nothing but the stub of every image behind the gap: the compiler put the whole library into the adapter, so there is no call the distance could reach.

  • Only the Cortex-M7 pays for the distance. On the NUCLEO-H723ZG a configuration that calls into its library costs 3 to 15 cycles more per operation with the cache on (median 6), and up to 66 more with it off. This is the step the experiment of the first report found between 2048 and 4096 bytes, now measured for every configuration.
  • A configuration that is inlined pays nothing, on any board. This includes configurations with code behind the gap that the single push and pop do not call (micoring's default mode; all modes of pxq, whose single-byte operations are inlined and whose block operations lie behind the gap). pxq is the owner's own library; the page has to say that its single-byte numbers contain no call while most others do.
  • On the four Cortex-M0+, M4 and M33 boards the series are the same within 2 cycles, except one configuration on the nRF5340 DK (+6).
  • Pico 2: the same with the cache on (largest difference 0.7). With the cache off one configuration moved, embassy-sync Pipe: push 67 cycles lower, pop 68 higher. With that cache off every instruction is fetched from the external flash, and the number follows where the code lies in it; nothing else moved.

Flash writes

48, as planned: nRF5340 DK 5, NUCLEO-G0B1RE 9, NUCLEO-G474RE 9, NUCLEO-H723ZG 5, SAM D20 18, Pico 2 2 (its first bundle in four parts, each sector once). On top of that, three writes of the Pico 2's first bundle that failed part way.

After the series: one more on the Pico 2, to try the write in parts as a tool on the board (the second bundle, 117 units, in two parts; the bundle was on the board afterwards).

What went wrong

  • The Pico 2 lost power during the flash write, three times. Cause found: while the probe writes, the board's own USB port shows up at the hub and does not answer; 65 s after that the kernel power-cycles the hub port ("attempt power cycle"), and the board, which is fed from that port, is gone in the middle of the write. A bundle of 4 MB takes longer than 65 s; 1 MB takes 25 to 30 s. The bundle was then written in four parts of about 1 MB, waiting for the power cycle between the parts; after it the runner found the whole bundle on the board and wrote nothing. This also explains the Pico 2 that "halted about 60 s after a download" in earlier notes. lab/tools/pico2-write.py now does this, before the runner is started; the runner itself is not changed, because it is one of the sources the records were made with (DECISIONS 2026-11-22). A cable that carries power only would remove the cause.
  • NUCLEO-H723ZG: two images were not finished 3 s after the reset and were run again with a longer wait (the runner's rule); numbers as in the other pass.

Reproduce
