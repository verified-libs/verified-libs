What was measured: what a pool is here, the pool's shape, what every call was checked for

A pool here is a library that hands out blocks of one size from storage fixed at start, with alloc (a block, or none left) and free. One pool shape is measured: 32 blocks of 32 bytes. Every number comes from a record under records/pools and is a fact about the pinned version, the configuration and the board it names, not a general ranking.

Every call was checked on the board, outside the timed window: a block is given exactly when fewer than 32 are held, lies in the pool at the promised alignment and is not held already, its pattern is intact at its free, the canaries around the pool are intact, and all blocks come back at the end (method). A run with a fault gives no record.

How to read the tables: the columns, the patterns, our two rows

Per board one fold-out: a table of bytes, init, and the pattern pair (alloc, then free of the same block, the pool almost empty: the common use in a driver), with the cache on and off; inside it a second fold-out with every pattern: a table of bytes and init, and one table per pattern. One row per configuration, in the order of kinds/pools/configs.txt; the order is not a ranking. Each configuration's name links to its record.

  • Cycles: the mean of the scenario's calls, the first call included, in CPU cycles with the counter-read overhead subtracted, as the range over eight placements of the code (min–max), first pass. Two rows differ only where their ranges do not overlap.
  • The scenarios: pair (alloc, then free of the same block, the pool almost empty), pair_full (the same with 31 of the 32 blocks held), fill_drain_lifo and fill_drain_fifo (32 allocs, then 32 frees in reverse or in the same order), mixed (a fixed-seed sequence, the pool between 25 % and 75 % full), empty (alloc on an exhausted pool, which must fail). init is one call.
  • Flash bytes: the library's and the adapter's code and constant data, measured in the board's own images at -O2 (what the image has that an image with a pool that does nothing has not; the kind's README, "Sizes"). RAM bytes: what the pool needs beyond the 32 blocks of 32 bytes and the canaries around them: its control data and any overhead per block.
  • Pin: the version measured (kinds/pools/libs.lock). MemoryPool is measured at a commit that is not a release; the method page says why.
  • Lock as built: what the library takes around a call in the build measured, from the kind's README.
  • Documented use from interrupts (not tested): the library's own words about use from interrupts or threads, with their source (kinds/pools/claims.tsv). This series does not test them.
  • Our two rows, not libraries: the reference free list with interrupts off, labelled in its name cell; and, as the last row of every table, the reference free list itself, the baseline timed in the same images.

Notes to the tables: MemoryPool's pin, heapless, C_Allocator, ThreadX

[1] MemoryPool is measured at its main branch, commit 5073178, which is not a release. The project's only release, v0.1.0, spaces the blocks of MemoryPool::Fixed by the size of a pointer, so blocks of 32 bytes overlap, and it does not build for arm-none-eabi; the author fixed the spacing in pull request #4 (merged 2024-05-14) and made the mutex optional (2024-05-15), with no release since (kinds/pools/README.md, "MemoryPool: why the head, not the release").

[2] A heapless Box cannot be made from a raw pointer, so the adapter keeps the live boxes in a table indexed by the block's place: a few instructions per call, and 128 of the RAM bytes. heapless is not built for the Cortex-M0+ (its documentation).

[3] C_Allocator treats an exhausted pool as a fault and calls FaultHandler(); the adapter's handler counts the call and returns, and ALLOC_Alloc then gives NULL (scenario empty).

[4] ThreadX runs without its scheduler (kinds/pools/tx_kernel_stubs.c); the stubs are never reached with TX_NO_WAIT. Its RAM bytes include a pointer in front of every block.

Each sentence names the rows it rests on; the rows are those of the tables above (open a board). Cycles are quoted as the tables give them, in whole cycles, alloc before free; whether two ranges overlap is judged on the records' unrounded means.

  • Not told apart from the reference free list. In pair, the alloc range of RIOT memarray overlaps the baseline's on all six boards, with the cache on and off (cache on: nRF5340 DK 10 against 10, Raspberry Pi Pico 2 H 10–11 against 11, NUCLEO-G474RE 17 against 17, NUCLEO-H723ZG 11–14 against 11, NUCLEO-G0B1RE 22 against 22, SAM D20 Xplained Pro 19 against 19); its free does not (nRF5340 DK, cache on: 57 against 9). No other library's pair figure overlaps the baseline's.
  • Interrupts off around the reference. Our row "reference free list, interrupts off" is the baseline's free list with interrupts off (PRIMASK) around each call; the two rows differ by that lock and by where their code lies (the baseline is in every image's fixed part). With the cache on, in pair, our row gives 14 and 12 against the baseline's 10 and 9 on the nRF5340 DK, 15–16 and 13 against 11 and 10 on the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 H, 24 and 25 against 11 and 11 on the NUCLEO-H723ZG, 28–31 and 28 against 22 and 21 on the NUCLEO-G0B1RE, and 26–27 and 25 against 19 and 18 on the SAM D20 Xplained Pro. On the NUCLEO-G474RE its alloc range begins 0.02 cycles above the baseline's (17–21 against 17), and its free range overlaps the baseline's (16–20 against 17).
  • A library's own lock is not measured apart. ThreadX and lwIP take interrupts off around a call, and CMSIS-RTX does on the two Cortex-M0+ boards (the column "Lock as built"). No build of a library without its lock was run, so a library's lock is in its figures together with the rest of its code, and two libraries with different locks differ in both.
  • The cache. With the cache off, every pair figure, the baseline's included, is higher than with the cache on on the nRF5340 DK, the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 H, the NUCLEO-G474RE and the NUCLEO-H723ZG. On the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 H, whose code is then read from the external QSPI flash on every fetch (CRC method, "What is measured"), each is 22 to 54 times its figure with the cache on (the baseline's alloc: 493 against 11). On the NUCLEO-G0B1RE and the SAM D20 Xplained Pro a cache-off figure is at most 1.28 and 1.19 times its cache-on figure, and the baseline's free is the same in both (21 and 18).
