MP stress campaign with coverage, all six boards (night 2026-11-17/18)

13 hours per board, rb-mp-stress at commit 515fc3e, one library per reset, 1,000,000 values per library and run. Prescaler 1-4 in rotation; at prescaler 3 and 4 half the runs use aimed interrupts and every run records where its interrupts landed (method: 2026-11-17-mp-coverage-prototype.md). The first campaign of this kind, and the last long one before version 1 (DECISIONS 2026-11-18).

No errors in any library in its documented mode, in any run on any board. A run is valid when the control (an SPSC buffer used by two producers or two consumers) was caught in it.

Reading

  • The aimed runs are the ones that test: they caught the control in every run on every board. With them the lowest median per boundary is 52 (heapless on the H723); on the other boards it is 433 or more. With random interrupts no library on any board had all its boundaries interrupted 10 times in every run.
  • Random runs at the slow rates add little. On the Pico 2 they are never valid, so half of its prescaler 3/4 time (58 runs of about 3 minutes) gave nothing.
  • The aim window is one value (160 cycles) for every library and board. The H723 gains least from aiming (not examined why), and the long lock-free operations (heapless, reserve/commit) keep boundaries below 10 on most boards. A window per library would close that; it is a method change and waits until after version 1.
  • Boundaries in blocks that were never interrupted (heapless: about 90 boundaries on the Cortex-M4/M33/M7 boards, 41 on the Cortex-M0+ ones) are paths the test does not take, likely retries after a real collision. An interrupt test on one core does not reach them; model checking does.

For the publication re-run

No change is made here. A proposal for the owner, since it changes what a campaign covers: at prescaler 3 and 4 run aimed interrupts only (random runs there are often invalid and never on the Pico 2), and keep random interrupts at prescaler 1 and 2, where the control is caught reliably.
