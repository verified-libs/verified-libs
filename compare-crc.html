Compare CRC calculation

The table holds the libraries and, on the boards that have one, the board's own CRC unit, driven by our code: it is not a library, and its name says so in every row. Its bytes are our adapter code only. On the Pico 2 the unit is a DMA transfer that computes the CRC on the way, and the CPU waits for it; for CRC-32 on the STM32 unit the final XOR is done in our code. With the unit shown, scores are relative to it where its value is the lowest; the filter "Kind of row" takes it out.

Pick a CRC, a board and a message, and set weights for what you care about. There is no ranking of ours: without weights the rows are in the order of the configuration list, and any order is the result of your weights, which are stored in the address so the view can be linked. Every number is the range over eight placements of the code (min–max), first pass, and so is the score. Choose a configuration's name to mark the rows whose range overlaps its range. A range is min–max over eight placements of the code. Two rows differ only where their ranges do not overlap. With a configuration chosen, "overlaps" marks the rows whose range overlaps its range in that column, compared as the numbers are shown: this series does not tell those rows apart from the chosen one. With weights, a score marked "overlaps above" overlaps the score of the row above it: this series does not tell the two apart. The score's marks are judged on the score before rounding, so two rows can show the same score without a mark when they differ by less than the rounding.

How the score is computed

With a weight above 0, each row gets a score from 0 to 100, as on the ring buffers' comparison: for each weighted value, the smallest value among the rows shown divided by the row's own, then the mean weighted by your weights, times 100, shown to one decimal. The rows are the libraries and, on the boards that have one, the board's CRC unit, scored like any row: with it shown, a score is relative to the unit's value wherever that value is the lowest. Choose "library" under "Kind of row" for the scores among the libraries only. On the nRF5340 DK and the SAM D20, and on the Pico 2 for CRC-8, no unit was measured, and a line above the table says so. The values are the cycles for one call at the chosen length, the cycles per byte, and the bytes of flash and RAM together (so a table built in RAM is not free). The cycles are ranges, so the score is a range too: the smallest value is the smallest lower end among the rows shown; a row's lowest score uses its own upper ends, its highest score its lower ends. Bytes are exact. The rows are ordered by the highest end of the score, ties in list order, and a score whose range overlaps the one above it is marked "overlaps above". The reference has no score, is never the smallest value a score is taken against, and stays apart as the last row. Sorting by name, flash or RAM bytes (the column headers) overrides the score order.

The marks are judged two ways, on purpose. The score's marks ("overlaps above", and "overlaps" in the score column for a chosen configuration) are judged on the score before rounding, so a mark never says "not told apart" where the series tells two rows apart, as with different bytes; two rows can therefore show the same score without a mark when they differ by less than the rounding. The marks in the cycles and per-byte columns are judged on the numbers as shown (whole cycles, cycles per byte to one decimal), so that two rows that show the same number are always marked.

What the score does not say: every row's results were checked against the reference on every call, so correctness is not a weight; nor are language, licence or how the library's API looks. Those are for you to judge, with the filters and the records.

What the columns are

Cycles: the mean of 256 calls (the first call included) on a message of the chosen length and start. Cycles per byte: (mean at 1024 bytes − mean at 256 bytes) / 768, at the chosen start. Flash bytes: the table in flash and the code at -O2 for the board's core; RAM bytes: the table in RAM. A table "RAM (copied at start by the image)" is a library's table that our build moved from flash to RAM; it counts in both, as its bytes stay in flash for the start-up code to copy. libcrc and CRC++ build theirs in RAM themselves. After the libraries, on the boards that have one, is the board's CRC unit (the STM32 CRC unit or the RP2350's DMA sniffer), driven by our code: it is not a library, its class is "hardware unit", it has no table, and its bytes are those of our adapter code. The last row, apart, is the reference, the bitwise algorithm written from the catalogue's parameters and timed in the same images: it is the baseline, not a library. The 1-byte messages are in the records and not here: at an 8-byte boundary with the cache on, that scenario is the first one after the cache is switched on. The second pass and the minimum, maximum and first call of each scenario are in the records, one per row.
