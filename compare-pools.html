Compare fixed-block memory pools

Measured in one context. The cycles are timed with no interrupt calling the pool. Whether a pool stays correct when an interrupt calls it is tested by a series of its own, the concurrency series (method). The column of what each library documents about use from interrupts gives its own words, with their source; no score uses it.

Pick a board, a scenario and the cache, and set weights for what you care about. There is no ranking of ours: without weights the rows are in the order of the configuration list, and any order is the result of your weights, which are stored in the address so the view can be linked. Every number is the range over eight placements of the code (min–max), first pass, and so is the score. Choose a configuration's name to mark the rows whose range overlaps its range. One pool shape: 32 blocks of 32 bytes. All numbers and how they were measured: Memory pool results and Method.

Measured in one context. Use from interrupts is not tested in this series; the last column is what each library documents.

A range is min–max over eight placements of the code. Two rows differ only where their ranges do not overlap. With a configuration chosen, "overlaps" marks the rows whose range overlaps its range in that column, compared as the numbers are shown (whole cycles): this series does not tell those rows apart from the chosen one. With weights, a score marked "overlaps above" overlaps the score of the row above it: this series does not tell the two apart. The score's marks are judged on the score before rounding, so two rows can show the same score without a mark when they differ by less than the rounding.

How the score is computed

With a weight above 0, each row gets a score from 0 to 100, as on the other comparison pages: for each weighted value, the smallest value among the rows shown divided by the row's own, then the mean weighted by your weights, times 100, shown to one decimal. The values are the cycles of one alloc and of one free in the chosen scenario, and the bytes of flash and RAM together. The cycles are ranges, so the score is a range too: the smallest value is the smallest lower end among the rows shown; a row's lowest score uses its own upper ends, its highest score its lower ends. Bytes are exact. A value a row does not have (no free in the scenario empty) counts 0. The rows are ordered by the highest end of the score, ties in list order, and a score whose range overlaps the one above it is marked "overlaps above". Our row with interrupts off is scored like any row; choose "library" under "Kind of row" for the scores among the libraries only. The reference free list has no score, is never the smallest value a score is taken against, and stays apart as the last row. Sorting by name, flash or RAM bytes (the column headers) overrides the score order.

What the score does not say: every call of every row was checked on the board (a block given exactly when one is free, in the pool, aligned, not given twice, its contents and the pool's canaries intact), so correctness is not a weight. Nor is what a library documents about interrupts, which this series does not test, nor language, licence, the lock or how the API looks. Those are for you to judge, with the filter and the records.

What the columns are

Cycles: the mean of the scenario's calls (the first call included), in CPU cycles less the counter-read overhead; init is one call that creates the pool. Version: the pin measured; MemoryPool is measured at a commit after its only release, which is not a release itself (why). Lock as built: what the library takes around a call in the build measured. Flash bytes: the library's and the adapter's code and constant data at -O2, measured in the board's images; RAM bytes: what the pool needs beyond the 32 blocks of 32 bytes and their canaries. Documented use from interrupts: the library's own words, with their source; what the concurrency series observed is beside it, and no score uses either. The row "reference free list, interrupts off (ours)" is our free list with interrupts off around each call: the price of the simplest lock on the core, not a library. The last row, apart, is the reference free list without a lock, timed in the same images: the baseline, not a library. The second pass and the minimum, maximum and first call of each scenario are in the records, one per row.
